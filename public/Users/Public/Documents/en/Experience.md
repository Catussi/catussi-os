### Junior Full Stack Developer

**March to June 2026**

ELVIR is a platform for practicing job interviews through a conversational avatar. It was built for a pilot with young people from Teletón and had to work in the browser — no install, on both desktop and mobile.

I worked across all three layers of the product. In Angular I implemented flows for young person, tutor, and administrator, session prep, the waiting room, and the follow-up panel. In FastAPI I built endpoints, validations, JWT authentication, and RBAC permissions. In PostgreSQL I maintained the relational model and its Alembic migrations.

The AI integration wasn't about training our own model. My responsibility was connecting and orchestrating external services — LiveAvatar, LiveKit, and ElevenLabs — inside the product experience: session creation, dynamic context, prompts from the research team, and Spanish voice.

I also prepared tests, healthchecks, technical documentation, and deployments with Docker, Vercel, Supabase, and Northflank. During the pilot I handled real incidents involving audio, browser permissions, and mobile compatibility.

**Outcome:** the platform was used in a real pilot with Teletón. The ITISB supervisor highlighted progress on both frontend and backend.

[Public demo](https://elvir-demo.vercel.app/) · [Code and documentation](https://github.com/Catussi/ELVIR-Demo)

### Computer Science Engineering Intern

**January to February 2026**

The internship started with an open-ended brief. I prepared mockups and user journeys, proposed a frontend/backend architecture, and designed the first data model. An important decision was separating the avatar's technical session from the job simulation, because they had different lifecycles and data.

I chose FastAPI for the API, defined the initial REST contracts, and implemented a first flow connected to LiveAvatar. The delivery included demo scripts, documentation, and a repository that became the base for continuing the project.

**Outcome:** after the prototype was validated, I stayed on the team as a junior full-stack developer.

## What I learned from these experiences

- A product isn't done when the code works: it also needs documentation, deployment, support, and an experience people can understand.
- External integrations require handling errors and intermediate states, not just calling an API.
- Working with real users changes priorities; compatibility, permissions, and clear messaging can matter as much as a new feature.
- I'd rather admit what I still don't know and study it than present a tool as solid expertise.
